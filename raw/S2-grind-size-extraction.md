- **Author / Publisher**: Alex Bidstrup / Perfect Daily Grind (December 1, 2022)

- **Status**: Read-only raw source

## Key Verified Facts
- **Experts Interviewed**: Professor Chahan Yeretzian (Head of Coffee Excellence Centre, Zurich University of Applied Sciences) and Scott Rao (coffee consultant & author).
- **Surface Area & Contact Time**:
  - **Coarser grind** = smaller surface area → requires longer contact time to extract flavors.
  - **Finer grind** = greater surface area → extracts faster, requiring shorter contact time to avoid bitter over-extraction (e.g., espresso 25–40 s vs. filter coffee 3–4 minutes).
- **Immersion vs. Percolation**:
  - **Immersion (浸泡式)**: Grounds stay in full contact with water throughout extraction → more pronounced mouthfeel.
  - **Percolation (濾泡式 / 如手沖)**: Water passes through the coffee bed → cleaner-tasting cup.
- **Sand & Pebbles Analogy & Channeling**:
  - Water passes faster through pebbles (coarse) than sand (fine).
  - Grinding **too fine** or having uneven particle distribution creates excessive resistance → water finds paths of least resistance (**channeling / 通道效應**), producing a simultaneous mix of under-extracted and over-extracted coffee.
- **Grind Uniformity & Equipment**:
  - Both Scott Rao and Prof. Chahan Yeretzian emphasize that **grind size uniformity (粒徑均勻度)** is the single most important factor regardless of coarse or fine setting, and grinder burr alignment strongly impacts extraction.
- **Noted Terminology Slip in Original Text**:
  - The article states *"The ideal TDS measurement for filter coffee is between 18% and 22%, whereas this percentage is lower for espresso – around 8% to 12%"*. (Note for Wiki Lint: 18–22% is actually filter coffee **Extraction Yield (PE)**, not TDS; filter TDS is ~1.15–1.35% per S1/SCA.)
