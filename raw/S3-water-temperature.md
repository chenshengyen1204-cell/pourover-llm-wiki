## Key Verified Facts
- **Target Water Temperature Range**: **195–205°F (90–96°C)** (SCA recommended range).
- **Solvent Science**: Water is a solvent whose extraction ability increases with temperature. Too hot pulls bitter tannins and chlorogenic acids (over-extraction: bitter, harsh, flat); too cool under-extracts (sour, weak, thin).
- **Adjusting by Roast Level**:
  - **Light roasts (淺焙)**: **200–205°F (~93–96°C)** — denser, lower moisture, harder to extract; needs more thermal energy.
  - **Medium roasts (中焙)**: **198–202°F (~92–94°C)** — balanced sweetness and body.
  - **Dark roasts (深焙)**: **195–198°F (~90–92°C)** — structurally broken down and more soluble; lower temperature avoids amplifying bitterness.
- **Practical Temperature Control & Altitude**:
  - Boiling water at sea level (212°F / 100°C) rested off heat for **30–45 seconds** drops to ~200–205°F; ~90 seconds approaches 195°F.
  - **Altitude effect**: At higher elevation (e.g., Denver at 5,280 ft), boiling point drops to ~202°F.
- **System Interactions & Troubleshooting**:
  - Starting ratio suggested by S3: **1:15** (1 g coffee per 15 g water).
  - **Sour / tart**: Under-extraction → raise temperature or lengthen bloom/brew time.
  - **Bitter / harsh**: Over-extraction → drop temperature 3–5°F or grind coarser.
  - **Flat / hollow**: Under-extraction or skipped/cool bloom.
  - **Watery but not sour**: Ratio issue (measure by weight, not volume).
  - **Golden Rule**: Change **one variable at a time**.
