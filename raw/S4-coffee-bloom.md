## Key Verified Facts
- **What Is Blooming**: Rapid release of trapped **CO₂** (produced during roasting) when hot water first touches freshly ground coffee, causing visible swelling and bubbling.
- **Physics & Chemistry**:
  - **Henry's Law (亨利定律)**: As water temperature rises, its ability to hold dissolved gases drops, forcing CO₂ out as bubbles.
  - Escaping CO₂ physically repels water from coffee grounds, causing **channeling** if not cleared before the main pour.
  - Dissolved CO₂ forms **carbonic acid (碳酸)**, contributing a sour, flat taste if left trapped.
- **Standard Bloom Recipe**:
  - Water weight: **2:1** water-to-coffee ratio (e.g., **40 g water for 20 g coffee**).
  - Water temperature: **195–205°F (90–96°C)**.
  - Wait time: **30–45 seconds** (45 s is a common sweet spot; extending past **60 seconds** drops slurry temperature noticeably).
  - Agitation: One **gentle swirl** evens saturation without driving fine particles into the filter (unlike vigorous stirring).
- **Roast Level & Freshness Diagnostics**:
  - **Light roasts**: Denser, hold more CO₂ → longer bloom time.
  - **Dark roasts / older beans**: Degas faster → shorter bloom time.
  - **Freshness peak**: **5–14 days post-roast**; vigorous bloom = fresh (1–2 weeks); weak/no bloom = stale (>3 weeks) or poor storage (use sealed bags with a **one-way valve**).
- **Flavor Impact**: Blind tastings (Serious Eats) consistently found bloomed coffee richer, rounder, with fuller body, cleaner aromatics, and reduced harsh acidity.
