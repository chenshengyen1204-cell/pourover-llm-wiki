## Key Verified Facts
- **Cone Dripper (e.g., Hario V60)**:
  - **Geometry**: 60° cone, one large drainage hole, spiral wall ribs.
  - **Flow Control**: Largely controlled by pour technique and grind size.
  - **Cup Profile**: Bright, defined acidity and clarity; sensitive to pouring errors or overly fine grinds.
- **Flat-Bottom Dripper (e.g., Kalita Wave, Orea O1)**:
  - **Geometry**: Flat base keeping uniform coffee bed depth from edge to center; Kalita Wave has **three small drainage holes** and a pleated wave filter that slows drainage and forgives imperfect pours.
  - **Cup Profile**: Balanced, sweet, repeatable cups with more even drawdown.
- **Hybrid**: **Origami Dripper M** (20 ribs) accepts both cone (V60-02) and flat-bottom (Kalita Wave #185) filters.
- **Lab Research (UC Davis Coffee Center & SCA, Frost, Ristenpart & Guinard, 2019, *Journal of Food Science*)**:
  - Compared flat-bottom vs. semi-conical baskets in automatic drip brewers with coffee, water, temperature, and flow rate held constant.
  - Semi-conical baskets produced significantly higher strength (**%TDS**).
  - On light roasts, **flat-bottom** highlighted **dried fruit, sweetness, and florals**, while **semi-conical** highlighted **citrus, berry, and sourness**.
  - Caveat: Tested on automatic drip baskets rather than hand pour-over; consumer preference split into groups.
- **Practical Dial-In**:
  - Flat-bottom starting grind: medium (**600–800 µm** on Orea); cone: slightly finer.
  - TOLO comparison ratio: **1:18 (20 g / 360 g)** with 60 g bloom and three pours.
